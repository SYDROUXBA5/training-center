The vapour law runs on kelvin

Evaporation follows the Clausius–Clapeyron relation, which divides by absolute temperature. Plug in Celsius and 0 °C would divide by zero.

P = P₂₅ × exp[−ΔH/R × (1/T − 1/298.15 K)]

Enthalpies: TNT and RDX from Hikal et al. (Talanta), 2,4-DNT from Pella, J. Chem. Thermodyn. 9:301 (1977), and PETN from Lau et al., J. Chem. Eng. Data 49:544 (2004). Room model. The plume near the aid is a particle sketch: airflow, sticking to surfaces and bead counts are illustrative, and the bead rate is set equal for every compound at 20 °C. The room average and timeline use a well-mixed box model: 68.6 m³ room, 50 cm² of exposed aid surface, a mass-transfer coefficient of 3 m/h, and 0.5 air changes per hour closed (2 with the door open, 5 with the window open, 15 with both). These are typical assumed values, not measurements, and uptake onto surfaces is ignored, so sticky compounds will read high. Vapour is extrapolated from 25 °C values with a constant enthalpy. “Share in the aid” scales vapour by mole fraction (Raoult’s law), a rough guide for trace compounds.

Molecules. 3D shapes are PubChem computed conformers, drawn ball-and-stick with carbon dark, hydrogen white, oxygen red and nitrogen blue. Inside the tin, escape rates are to scale between the compounds shown, and the crystal is a simplified cubic packing. No reliable canine detection threshold has been published for these compounds, so none is drawn.
